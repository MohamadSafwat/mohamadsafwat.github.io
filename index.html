Most distributors don’t lose money on one big mistake.

They lose it in the gaps — the same item bought at three different prices, a supplier who quietly became 60% of purchasing, an order that was never delivered at all. Standard reports show totals and averages. That’s exactly where these gaps hide.

I run procurement and operations diagnostics for distribution businesses, and build the systems that close what the analysis finds.

Case study

A supply rate of 72.8% concealed a fulfilment failure

A national health authority in the MENA region was tracking a single procurement metric: 72.8% of demand supplied. Stable, unremarkable, and wrong about the thing that mattered.

The finding

That 72.8% was an average of two extremes. Orders were either fulfilled completely or not at all — 44% of repeat orders received nothing. Almost no partial deliveries. Not random under-supply; a systemic failure the headline metric was smoothing over.

What it added up to

27%of annual procurement value unfulfilled
44%of repeat orders received nothing
59suppliers analysed across 1,233 items

What it meant

The unfulfilled orders were not spread evenly across the catalogue. They concentrated in items with no clinical substitute — anticoagulants, anaesthetics, antibiotics. The organisation was not slightly under-supplied. It was fully supplied on some things and entirely without others.

What was recommended

Supplier segmentation by fulfilment performance rather than spend. Inventory policy rebuilt around item criticality instead of value. Forecasting adjusted for historical supplier reliability, so the plan reflected expected supply rather than demand alone.

Method

How a diagnostic runs

  1. Start with the data you already have

    Twelve months of purchase records in a spreadsheet or an export from your system. No new software, no integration project.

  2. Find where the money and the reliability are going

    Price variance on identical items, supplier concentration, discount leakage, fulfilment failure, capital locked in slow-moving stock.

  3. Separate the symptoms from the causes

    A supplier missing deliveries and a category losing margin often share one root — usually a process, not a person.

  4. Hand over something usable

    A written analysis, an executive presentation, and a prioritised list of what to change first.

Six weeks, typically. Longer if the data needs rebuilding first.
