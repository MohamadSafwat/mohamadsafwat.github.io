Sales up almost sixfold. Margin at a six-year low.

I have run a medical-supplies distribution business since 2018. It grew fast on a handful of big accounts and a new product line. So I put it through the same diagnostic I run for clients — and six years of accounts showed where the growth went.

THE QUESTION

We were selling more than ever. Why wasn't more of it staying with us?

Sales had grown almost sixfold in five years, most of it in the last two. I could see the top line; I could not see why profit per pound of sales had halved. So I treated my own company as a client: six years of accounts rebuilt into one view — by customer, supplier, category and cost line — to find where the growth was leaking.

It is the same method, and the same honesty, I bring to every engagement.

WHAT THE DATA SHOWED

Growth was real. Profit didn't follow.

89%

Of gross profit eaten by expenses

In the last year expenses grew 45% while gross profit grew 16%. Most of every margin point earned was spent before it reached the bottom line.

+35%

Salaries in one year — and delivery costs doubled

The two biggest cost lines moved fastest. Delivery to the furthest routes rose 67–93% in a year.

47 → 15

Active customers, while order size grew 8×

Growth came from bigger orders from fewer accounts — efficient, until one of them leaves.

5 + 5

Customers and suppliers holding the business

Five customers took 70% of sales; five suppliers 67% of purchases. Two suppliers alone were over half.

~4%

Margin on the new line that drove the growth

A new product line added most of the growth at about 4% contribution — while the established line stood still.

60 → 52

The biggest categories earned less than their share

The top three categories carried 60% of sales but only 52% of profit: volume was being bought with margin.

WHAT I RECOMMENDED

Four pillars, each with a number to hit

01

Fortify the core

Expenses back under 60% of gross profit: a cost review on the costliest delivery routes, salary rises tied to a performance review, and renegotiated terms on the top three categories.

02

Grow the new line — profitably

+45% on the new line, with collections kept tight and its margin held, not traded for volume.

03

Restart the established line

A reorganised sales team with clear accounts, and margin and receivables fixed at the two largest customers.

04

De-risk

Two new large accounts or four smaller ones, and one or two new suppliers for the key categories.

THE KEY RATIO

Expenses as a share of gross profit

Above 100% means the business spent more running itself than it earned on its sales. The ratio swung every year and never came close to a level that leaves real profit. The plan sets one target and tracks it monthly.
