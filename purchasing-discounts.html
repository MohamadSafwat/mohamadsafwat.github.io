THE QUESTION

Are we getting the discount we should, from everyone we buy from?

Purchasing was growing fast and every buyer negotiated their own terms. Nobody could say what a good discount looked like for a given item, or which vendors were costing the most. I used the group's own best results as the yardstick — the terms it had already achieved somewhere — and measured everything else against them.

WHAT I RECOMMENDED

Buy like one business, not three stores

01

Standardise discounts

A target discount per item and vendor, using the best store as the benchmark. Closing its 3.2-point lead alone recovers a large share of the gap.

02

Rebalance the vendor mix

A scorecard for every vendor — spend, discount, gap. Renegotiate with the three biggest gaps first; move volume to vendors that already give better terms.

03

Consolidate and plan

Fold small bills into quarterly agreements and spread buying across the month with a simple forecast, instead of a last-week rush.

WHERE TO START

Ten vendors hold three-quarters of the opportunity

The savings are not spread evenly. Three negotiations cover half of the total; ten cover three-quarters. That makes the plan short and the first wins quick.
