Sales up 23% — and the business losing money

WHAT THE DATA SHOWED

Six findings — none of them visible in the reports the owner was reading

+46%

The bar moved, not the sales

Staff, marketing, delivery and head-office costs all stepped up at once in June. The sales needed to break even rose by almost half.

+3% vs +19%

Growth from dearer items, not more customers

Visits were almost flat; most of the growth came from a handful of high-value items — growth that can disappear as fast as it came.

1.21×

Buying ahead of sales

Bought 1.21 for every 1 sold in the last month, much of it in bulk deals bought without a calculation — cash turning into shelf stock.

96%

Suppliers financing the shelves

The chain owed suppliers almost the full value of its stock, and the cheques written for it were falling due week after week.

2.3×

Delivery cost per order

Riders and a call center added before the orders came: by August a delivery order earned nothing, and six in ten were too small to pay their way.

1 in 3

Customers slipping away unseen

One chronic patient in three was late for a refill, and hundreds of former regulars had stopped buying — nothing in the system flagged them.

Where the profit comes back from
