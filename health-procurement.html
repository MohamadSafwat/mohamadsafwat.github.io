A 72.8% supply rate that hid an all-or-nothing failure

A national health authority tracked its pharmaceutical supply rate at 72.8% — a number that looked stable. Ten months of orders told a different story: most items arrived in full, or not at all.

THE QUESTION

Why do hospitals still run out, when 73% of everything ordered arrives?

Leadership saw one monthly number — the supply rate — and it had been steady. Clinicians were reporting stockouts of essential medicines at the same time. My job was to reconcile the two: find where the missing 27% actually sat, which items and suppliers it came from, and what would close it.

WHAT THE DATA SHOWED

The average was hiding two extremes

27%

Of demand value never arrived

Behind a supply rate that looked stable, more than a quarter of what was ordered was not delivered — a direct risk to operations, not a rounding error.

44%

Of repeat orders received nothing

For routine, recurring items — the ones that should be easiest — delivery was close to a coin toss.

9%

Of orders were ever partly filled

Suppliers either delivered everything or nothing. That pattern points to a systemic cause — contracts, approvals, supplier capacity — not random shortage.

40%

Of items were never delivered at all

Including life-saving medicines with no clinical substitute — anticoagulants, antibiotics, anaesthetics — where a stockout hurts most.

Half

Of the gap came from items that never arrived once

1,607 orders with zero delivery made up about half of the total gap. Fix those, and most of the problem goes with them.

5 of 59

Suppliers carried 70% of delivered value

Two of them alone delivered nearly 40% — a single point of failure in the supply of essential medicines.

WHAT I RECOMMENDED

Three pillars, in three phases over twelve months

01

Segment suppliers

All 59 suppliers placed on value × reliability. Strategic partners get joint plans and preferred terms; under-performers get SLAs, a dashboard — or an alternative source.

02

Stock by clinical criticality

Every item classified by value and criticality (ABC × VEN). Vital items get higher safety stock and pre-approved alternative suppliers; forecasts adjusted for each supplier's real reliability.

03

Early warning

Live tracking of every open order, alerts on orders at risk of non-delivery, and a mandatory root-cause review for every zero delivery.

PROJECTED IMPACT

More than half the gap, recaptured

Each pillar closes a different part of the gap. Supplier management does the most; inventory policy protects the vital items first; process changes stop the same failures recurring.

Supply gap indexed to 100; projected reduction by pillar over 12 months.
